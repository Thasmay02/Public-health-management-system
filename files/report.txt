    \Huge \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.5cm}

\fontfamily{ptm}\selectfont % Explicitly setting Times New Roman for the Abstract
\noindent Health Base is a comprehensive, centralized Public Health Management System (PHMS) developed to modernize the administrative and clinical operations of public health facilities. Traditionally, health departments rely on manual record-keeping and fragmented software, which leads to delayed disease reporting, inaccurate patient histories, and inefficient resource allocation. To address these critical gaps, Health Base provides an automated, secure, and highly responsive digital infrastructure. The system is built as a robust full-stack web application, utilizing Node.js and Express.js for a fast RESTful API backend, MongoDB for flexible data persistence, and HTML5, CSS3, and Vanilla JavaScript for a responsive frontend interface. The platform integrates several major functionalities, including secure patient and user management, real-time disease and recovery tracking, automated clinical appointment scheduling, and a dedicated inventory module to monitor essential medical supplies. Furthermore, an overarching Administrator portal provides healthcare administrators with analytical insights and centralized control over facility operations. Ultimately, the successful implementation of Health Base establishes an efficient, paperless environment that significantly reduces human error and operational overhead, allowing healthcare providers to access critical data instantly and focus entirely on improving patient care and outbreak response capabilities.

\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent Keywords: Public Health Management System, PHMS, Web Application, Node.js, Express.js, MongoDB, Digital Infrastructure, Healthcare Administration.

\chapter{INTRODUCTION}

\section{Project Introduction}
The Health Base -- Public Health Management System (PHMS) is a comprehensive, centralized web application developed to overcome the critical limitations of manual, paper-based healthcare administration by providing a highly secure, role-based digital infrastructure that seamlessly integrates real-time patient registration, automated clinical appointment scheduling, proactive disease surveillance, and dynamic medical inventory tracking, thereby empowering medical professionals to eliminate administrative bottlenecks, drastically reduce human error, and redirect their primary focus toward delivering efficient, high-quality patient care. By transitioning from fragmented legacy software to a unified digital ecosystem, the system guarantees that patient medical histories are always up-to-date and instantly accessible from any authorized device. This real-time accessibility plays a crucial role in enabling faster, more accurate clinical diagnoses and improving the overall quality of care. Furthermore, the platform's robust architecture utilizes modern web technologies like Node.js and MongoDB to ensure high scalability and uninterrupted performance even during periods of heavy administrative load. It also introduces an intuitive, role-based dashboard system that provides customized views for administrators, doctors, and patients, ensuring strict data privacy and simplified navigation. Ultimately, the Health Base initiative represents a vital technological leap forward in public health management, fostering a paperless environment that significantly enhances operational transparency and proactive outbreak response capabilities.

\section{Problem Statement}
Many local health departments and clinics continue to operate using manual, paper-based record-keeping or disjointed legacy software that cannot communicate effectively. This fragmented approach introduces severe systemic vulnerabilities, including a high risk of catastrophic data loss, inaccurate or incomplete patient medical histories, and dangerous delays in identifying and reporting infectious disease outbreaks. Furthermore, manual appointment scheduling leads to congested waiting rooms and extended wait times, while the lack of an automated inventory tracking mechanism frequently results in unnotified shortages of life-saving medical supplies. There is a pressing need for a unified, secure digital ecosystem to eliminate these persistent inefficiencies, allowing healthcare professionals to focus entirely on patient care rather than cumbersome administrative paperwork.

\section{Objectives}
The primary objective of this project is to design and develop a scalable, centralized platform that automates and secures core healthcare administration. The specific objectives are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item To create a highly secure, centralized database for storing detailed patient profiles, medical histories, and staff credentials.
    \item To implement an automated appointment booking system that drastically reduces patient wait times and improves clinical scheduling efficiency.
    \item To track disease occurrences and patient recovery rates in real-time, aiding administrators in proactive epidemiological responses.
    \item To provide an integrated inventory tracking module to actively monitor medical supplies and prevent critical stock shortages.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope of the Project}
The scope of the Health Base (PHMS) project encompasses the end-to-end digitization of core administrative and clinical tracking functions for a standard health clinic. The web-based system is designed with a secure, role-based architecture tailored specifically for Administrators, Doctors, and Patients. Regarding in-scope functionalities, the system provides comprehensive administrative oversight, including the management of facility users, real-time disease trend monitoring, and inventory tracking for medical supplies. For clinical workflows, it ensures secure access for doctors to update patient diagnoses, prescribe treatments, and manage their daily appointment schedules. Additionally, the project features a dedicated patient portal that allows patients to register, seamlessly book appointments, and view their personal medical history. On the other hand, certain elements remain out of scope for this initial phase. The project is strictly bounded to software implementation and internal facility management. It does not encompass direct integration with external hardware devices, such as IoT vitals monitors, nor does it cover automated financial billing systems or third-party insurance claim processing. However, the platform is built upon a highly modular and scalable technological foundation, ensuring that future integrations—such as telemedicine video consultations or secure API connectivity with national health registries—can be seamlessly incorporated.

\chapter{SYSTEM DESIGN}

\section{System Architecture}
The system architecture of the Health Base platform is based on a modern, three-tier client-server model designed for scalability and secure data processing. The presentation tier consists of a web-based frontend built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, providing an intuitive dashboard for Administrators, Doctors, and Patients to interact with the system. The application logic tier is powered by a Node.js server running the Express.js framework, which handles API requests, user authentication, and business logic for modules such as appointment scheduling and inventory tracking. Finally, the data tier utilizes MongoDB, a NoSQL database, to securely store and manage structured medical records, user credentials, and clinical data. This modular architecture ensures separation of concerns, allowing for independent updates to the user interface or database schema without disrupting the overall functionality of the platform.



\section{Data Flow Diagram (DFD)}
A Data Flow Diagram (DFD) visually represents how information moves within the Health Base system. It illustrates the primary pathways of data exchange between external entities (such as patients, doctors, and administrators) and the central system processes. By mapping these interactions, the DFD provides a clear understanding of how critical inputs, like user registrations and clinical diagnoses, are transformed into actionable outputs, such as appointment confirmations and analytical reports. Furthermore, this high-level data flow model serves as the foundational blueprint for the system's underlying RESTful API and database schema architecture. By strictly defining the boundaries of data exchange for each user role, the DFD ensures that the application enforces rigorous data validation and strict role-based access control. This structured flow of information is essential for maintaining robust data integrity, preventing unauthorized data access, and ensuring that all clinical and administrative workflows operate synchronously without creating bottlenecks or data silos.

As depicted in Figure \ref{PHMS.png}, the system flow logically sequences the user journey from the initial authentication gateway into the parallel execution of core modules like user management, disease tracking, and inventory control. This structured visualization ensures that all user sessions strictly follow a validated path before accessing sensitive data, ultimately concluding with a secure logout process.

\section{Database Seeding Script}

As detailed in \textbf{Listing \ref{lst:seed_script}}, the following Node.js script is used to populate the MongoDB database with initial records such as users, patients, appointments, diseases, medical records, and inventory items. This simplifies testing and ensures that the application starts with sample data.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=JavaScript, caption={MongoDB Database Seeding Script}, label={lst:seed_script}, numbers=left]
require('dotenv').config();
const mongoose = require('mongoose');
const fs = require('fs');
const path = require('path');

const User = require('./models/User');
const Patient = require('./models/Patient');
const Appointment = require('./models/Appointment');
const Record = require('./models/Record');
const Disease = require('./models/Disease');
const Inventory = require('./models/Inventory');

const DB_FILE = path.join(__dirname, 'db.json');

async function seed() {
  try {
    await mongoose.connect(
      process.env.MONGODB_URI ||
      'mongodb://127.0.0.1:27017/phms'
    );

    console.log('Connected to MongoDB');

    if (!fs.existsSync(DB_FILE)) {
      console.log('db.json not found');
      process.exit(0);
    }

    const data = JSON.parse(
      fs.readFileSync(DB_FILE, 'utf8')
    );

    if (data.users) {
      await User.deleteMany({});
      await User.insertMany(data.users);
    }

    if (data.patients) {
      await Patient.deleteMany({});
      await Patient.insertMany(data.patients);
    }

    if (data.appointments) {
      await Appointment.deleteMany({});
      await Appointment.insertMany(data.appointments);
    }

    if (data.records) {
      await Record.deleteMany({});
      await Record.insertMany(data.records);
    }

    if (data.diseases) {
      await Disease.deleteMany({});
      await Disease.insertMany(data.diseases);
    }

    if (data.inventory) {
      await Inventory.deleteMany({});
      await Inventory.insertMany(data.inventory);
    }

    console.log('Database seeding completed.');
    process.exit(0);

  } catch (err) {
    console.error(err);
    process.exit(1);
  }
}

seed();
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Backend API Implementation}
The backend logic is driven by \textbf{Node.js} running the \textbf{Express.js} framework within the \texttt{phms-backend-server.js} file. The server acts as a central hub, exposing secure RESTful endpoints protected by JSON Web Tokens (JWT) and bcrypt password hashing for stringent user authentication. The authentication API handles secure user login and role assignment through endpoints such as \texttt{/api/auth/login}. In addition, the data management API provides various routes for handling core entities, permitting Create, Read, Update, and Delete (CRUD) operations on Patients, Appointments, Medical Records, Inventory, and Diseases. The server also integrates standard middlewares, including \texttt{helmet} for enhanced HTTP header security, \texttt{cors} for cross-origin resource sharing, and \texttt{morgan} for detailed API logging. \textbf{Listing \ref{lst:backend_api}} provides a structural overview of the Express.js server and the secure authentication endpoint.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=JavaScript, caption={Express.js Server and Authentication Endpoint}, label={lst:backend_api}, numbers=left]
const express = require('express');
const cors = require('cors');
const helmet = require('helmet');
const morgan = require('morgan');
const jwt = require('jsonwebtoken');
const bcrypt = require('bcryptjs');

const app = express();

// Security and Logging Middleware
app.use(helmet());
app.use(cors({ origin: '*', credentials: true}));
app.use(morgan('dev'));
app.use(express.json());

// Secure User Authentication Endpoint
app.post('/api/auth/login', async (req, res)=>{
  try {
    const { email, password } = req.body;
    const user = await User.findOne({email});
    
    if (user && await bcrypt.compare(password, user.password)){
      const token = jwt.sign(
        { id: user._id, role: user.role }, 
        process.env.JWT_SECRET, 
        { expiresIn: '8h' }
      );
      res.json({ token, role: user.role, name: user.name});
    } else {
      res.status(401).json({ error: 'Invalid credentials'});
    }
  } catch (error) {
res.status(500).json({error:'Server error during authentication'});
  }
});
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Database Connectivity}
The application leverages \textbf{MongoDB}, a robust NoSQL database, to persistently store and retrieve operational data. The backend server establishes a secure connection to the database utilizing the \textbf{Mongoose} Object Data Modeling (ODM) library via the \texttt{mongoose.connect()} method, which points directly to the configured MongoDB connection URI. The data schema is meticulously structured using various Mongoose models, including \texttt{User}, \texttt{Patient}, \texttt{Appointment}, \texttt{Record}, \texttt{Disease}, and \texttt{Inventory}. These models define the shape of the data and ensure strict data validation as well as structural consistency before any information is persistently written to the database. \textbf{Listing \ref{lst:db_connectivity}} illustrates the concise setup required to establish this persistent database connection using Mongoose.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=JavaScript, caption={MongoDB Connection Setup}, label={lst:db_connectivity}, numbers=left]
// Establish secure connection to MongoDB
mongoose.connect(process.env.MONGODB_URI||'mongodb://127.0.0.1:27017/phms')
.then(() => console.log('Connected to MongoDB successfully'))
.catch(err => console.error('MongoDB connection error:',err));
\end{lstlisting}

\chapter{CONCLUSION}
The development of the Health Base – Public Health Management System (PHMS) successfully addresses the critical inefficiencies inherent in traditional, paper-based healthcare administration. By transitioning to a robust, digital platform, the project demonstrates the vast potential of modern web technologies to streamline clinical operations. The integration of secure role-based portals, automated appointment scheduling, and real-time medical inventory tracking provides a unified environment where healthcare professionals can operate with unprecedented efficiency. Furthermore, the system’s ability to securely manage patient records and instantly generate analytical reports ensures that critical data is always available for rapid decision-making during public health events. Ultimately, Health Base serves as a foundational step toward a fully modernized, paperless healthcare infrastructure that drastically reduces human error, optimizes resource allocation, and allows medical staff to dedicate their primary focus to delivering high-quality patient care.

\chapter{FUTURE ENHANCEMENTS}
While the current iteration of the Health Base system provides a robust foundation for clinic management, several avenues exist for future expansion. A primary enhancement would be the integration of an AI-driven predictive analytics module, which could utilize historical medical data to forecast potential disease outbreaks and intelligently automate supply restocking. Additionally, developing a dedicated cross-platform mobile application would significantly increase accessibility, allowing patients to manage their profiles and appointments directly from their smartphones. To further improve patient care, a telemedicine module could be introduced to facilitate secure remote video consultations and continuous health monitoring via IoT wearable devices. Finally, integrating an automated SMS and email notification system would ensure that patients receive timely reminders for upcoming appointments, while secure API connectivity with national health registries could seamlessly synchronize public health data on a broader scale.
